\textcolor{red}{Optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) metrics are widely used to assess the retinal vasculature, and vessel diameter has been linked to a range of vascular diseases.} However, shear-induced alignment of red blood cells at vessel edges reduces backscattering and suppresses the OCTA signal.~\textcolor{red}{Which can lead} to systematic underestimation of vessel diameter. In this study, we established ground-truth diameters in the in vivo mouse retina using Intralipid contrast enhancement, compared \textcolor{red}{conventional binary-mask-based} calculation against model-based profile fitting, and used flicker stimulation to evaluate the performance of each method.~\textcolor{red}{We additionally investigated these methods on maximum and mean intensity projection data to determine the best configuration.} Before contrast, a generalised Gaussian fit to the maximum intensity projection, \textcolor{red}{although underestimated diameter by approximately 35\%,} retained the strongest linear relationship with ground truth of all methods tested, making it correctable by calibration. Post-contrast, the same fit applied to the mean intensity projection \textcolor{red}{was the most reliable in our evaluation}. Under flicker stimulation, model-based fitting detected the expected vasodilation, whereas \textcolor{red}{binary-mask-based} calculation \textcolor{red}{was more affected by intensity fluctuations and} instead reported vasoconstriction. These findings characterise OCTA diameter underestimation and establish model-based fitting as a practical means of correcting it.
