Optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) metrics are widely used to assess the retinal vasculature, and vessel diameter has been linked to a range of vascular diseases. However, shear-induced alignment of red blood cells at vessel edges reduces backscattering and suppresses the OCTA signal, which can lead to systematic underestimation of vessel diameter. In this study, we established ground-truth diameters in the in vivo mouse retina using Intralipid contrast enhancement, compared conventional binary-mask-based calculation against model-based profile fitting, and used flicker stimulation to evaluate the performance of each method. We additionally investigated these methods on maximum and mean intensity projection data to determine the best configuration. Before contrast, a generalised Gaussian fit to the maximum intensity projection underestimated diameter by approximately 35\%, but retained the strongest linear relationship with ground truth of all methods tested, making it correctable by calibration. Post-contrast, the same fit applied to the mean intensity projection was the most reliable in our evaluation. In a test of flicker stimulation induced vasodilation, model-based fitting detected the expected vasodilation, whereas binary-mask-based calculation was more affected by intensity fluctuations and instead reported vasoconstriction. These findings characterise OCTA diameter underestimation and establish model-based fitting as a practical means of correcting it.
